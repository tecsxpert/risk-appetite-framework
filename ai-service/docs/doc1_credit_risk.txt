Credit Risk Appetite Framework

Credit risk is the potential for loss due to a borrower's failure to repay a loan or meet contractual obligations. Organizations must define their credit risk appetite clearly to guide lending decisions.

Key components of credit risk appetite include:
- Maximum acceptable default rate: typically 2-5% for retail lending
- Concentration limits: no single borrower should exceed 10% of total portfolio
- Credit score thresholds: minimum score of 650 for retail loans
- Loan-to-value ratios: maximum 80% for mortgage lending

Credit risk monitoring requires regular review of:
- Non-performing loan ratios
- Credit rating migrations
- Industry concentration metrics
- Geographic concentration metrics

Best practices for credit risk management include stress testing portfolios quarterly, maintaining adequate loan loss provisions, and implementing early warning systems for deteriorating credits.
